The difference

Two skills, and almost every course only teaches the second one.

Knowing how to use an AI tool is not the same as knowing which problem is worth pointing it at. This programme deliberately spends its first five months on the first skill, before anyone is allowed to build anything.

Step one  /  Identify

Find where the time actually goes

Your apprentice audits real working time in 30 minute blocks, maps how the work genuinely happens rather than how the process document says it does, and puts a cost against it.

  • Duplicated data entry between systems that never got connected
  • Work sitting in an inbox for two days before anyone picks it up
  • Reports rebuilt by hand every month
  • Information trapped in a spreadsheet only one person understands
  • Processes where AI is genuinely the wrong answer, and saying so
Step two  /  Solve

Build the fix, then prove it worked

They design it around the people who actually use it, build it in low-code and no-code tools, test it properly, get your team using it, and report what it saved in real numbers.

  • A working build by month three, not month fifteen
  • The main automation live in your systems by month ten
  • Documented and handed over, so it survives them leaving
  • Governance, security and monitoring built in, not bolted on
  • A costed report by month twelve showing what it actually delivered

The funding

Up to 100% of the cost is paid by the UK government.

This is not a grant you apply for and might not get. It is how apprenticeship funding already works, and this qualification sits inside it. Which of the three applies to you depends on your payroll and the age of the person you put forward.

Apprentice under 25, payroll under £3m 100% You pay nothing

Fully funded by the government. For most small and medium businesses putting forward a younger member of staff, there is no fee to you at all.

Payroll under £3m 95% You cover 5%

The standard position for most SMEs. The government funds 95% of the cost and you contribute the remaining 5%.

Levy payer, payroll over £3m Levy Money you already pay

This comes out of a pot you already pay into every month. Unspent levy funds now expire after 12 months, so if you do not spend it on training like this, you lose it.

Funding band for this standard is up to £18,000. If you are a levy payer whose pot for the year is already exhausted, co-investment on anything beyond it is 25% rather than 5%. Worth checking before you assume either figure.

The obvious questions

What business owners actually ask.

What is the catch, if the government pays?

There is not one. This is exactly what apprenticeship funding exists for and has done for years. This particular standard is just new. You are not the exception, you are precisely who it is for.

We cannot spare anyone for fifteen months.

It is not full time. It is around seven hours a week, and most of that is the project work someone would be doing anyway if this problem were being solved properly. You are not losing a person, you are pointing one at a problem you already have.

We already use ChatGPT or Copilot.

So does most of the market, and most of it reports no measurable gain. Using a tool and knowing how to build something durable and governed with it are different skills. This is the difference between someone who prompts and someone who ships.

What if the process turns out not to be automatable?

Then you will know that for certain instead of guessing, which is worth having on its own. Recommending against automation where it will not help is explicitly part of what they are assessed on, and there is a second process ready to go.

What if they finish and then leave?

Around two thirds of apprentices stay with the employer who trained them. And even where they do not, the automation stays: it is built inside your systems, documented and handed over before anyone goes anywhere.

We are too small for this.

Smaller businesses are exactly who it is built for, and the under 25 rule means many pay nothing. Size is not the barrier. Having a real problem worth solving is the requirement, and most businesses have several.
